How it works — layers with a weights layer on top

  1. Stars each: 1, 2 or 3 stars in every row, column and region (3 stars need at least a 12×12 board).
  2. The rules are Star Battle's: every row, column and region gets its stars, and no two stars touch. Each cell also has a cost (the number in its corner; darker = costlier). Many placements follow the rules — we want the one with the lowest total cost, like choosing the best sites to build on.
  3. Option layers with costs: every unit's options (ways to place its stars) now carry a cost — the sum of their cells' weights. The layer rules still cut options: stars can't touch, a full unit empties its other cells, a unit with one option left is locked.
  4. Lower bound: every star lies in exactly one row, so the total can't be less than the sum of each row's cheapest option. The same holds for columns and for regions — the method uses the highest of the three.
  5. Givens (optional): some stars are already placed (dark) and must stay — like sites that already exist. They come from a valid placement, not necessarily the cheapest, so the cheapest total with them can be higher; the page shows both.
  6. Cheapest first: branch on the unit with the fewest options, trying its cheapest option first — the first complete placement is usually already good.
  7. Weight descent (a better bound): put a price on every column and region, let each row simply take its cheapest cells under weight + prices, then adjust the prices step by step like gradient descent — a column with too many stars gets dearer, one with too few gets cheaper. Every set of prices gives a lower bound, and the descent pushes it up, so more branches are cut.
  8. ⚡ Cheapest-first (your rule): put a star on the cheapest free cell of the whole board, let the layer rules empty its neighbours and fill in what follows; if a row, column or region can then no longer get its stars, that cell stays empty and the next cheapest is tried. Instant, and much closer to the cheapest than a random start.
  9. 🔒 Lock cheapest (your rule): go through the cells from the cheapest up; lock a star on a free cell only if your layer method can still fit every other star with it (it finds a full valid placement), otherwise that cell stays empty. Locked stars are never revisited — like a locked layer — so nothing is undone and the result is always valid. Look ahead: before locking, try the 3 or 6 cheapest free cells, finish the placement from each with the cheapest-first rule, and lock the one whose finished total is lowest — much closer to the cheapest.
  10. ⛰ Downhill + Unstick: from the cheapest-first placement (or a random one), keep swapping the columns of two stars whenever that lowers the cost — until stuck in a valley. Then Unstick: free about 60% of the rows (or columns), keep the other stars, and let your layer method rebuild the freed band at the cheapest cost — a huge, always-valid move. Repeat with other bands. Fast and usually close to the cheapest, but only the bound can prove it.
  11. Branch menu: the proof search can branch on the unit with the fewest options, or on your rule — the cheapest free cell (star there, or not). Both always prove the cheapest; which is faster depends on the board.
  12. Cut by the bound: once a placement costing C is found, any branch whose lower bound is already C or more can't beat it and is cut without looking inside. When no branch is left, the best placement found is proved to be the cheapest.

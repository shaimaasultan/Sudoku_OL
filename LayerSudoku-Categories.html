Group puzzles by the language needed to solve them, and find what each group has in common · S. S. Soltan

Make and measure puzzles

Levels rotate between Easy, Medium and Hard. Each puzzle is solved with the Layer Builder (fewest places first) and measured.

Group by

Slice view

One puzzle of a group as a cube: rows × columns × numbers, one horizontal slice per number. Move the slider to replay how option layers locked the slices, one by one (grey pillars = the cells the slice just filled). Label: options built for that slice · 🔒 lock position (? = locked by a guess).

Group slice profile

Slices ranked thinnest first (the order option layers opens them: most repeated, ties by fewest free cells in a box), averaged over the group's puzzles.

What each group has in common

A feature is named when the group's average differs clearly from all puzzles (orange = higher, blue = lower), or when every puzzle in the group shares it.

Group averages

Features measured

Layer signature
For each number, the moves its layer needs from the home grid (one per box where its cell differs), with numbers renamed to their closest home layers; sorted, e.g. 7·7·6·6·5·5·5·4·3.
Total, spread, most-moved
Sum of the signature; largest minus smallest; largest.
Option layers
The Option Layers method, starting with the 2 most repeated numbers and adding the next when stuck: how many option layers it built, how many numbers were locked before it had to guess, and how many guesses it needed (0 = the AND cuts alone solved it). When every number is built and the pair check is stuck, it first checks the unlocked numbers 3 (then 4) at a time; "Triple/four cuts" counts how often that was needed.
Difficulty level A / B / C / D
A: your layer method finishes it. B: singles finish it. C: singles stall, but option layers finish it with no guessing. D: even option layers need a guess.
Difficulty level A / B / C
A: your layer method alone finishes every layer. B: it stalls, but singles finish every layer. C: singles stall too, so guessing (going back) is needed.
Choice layers
On the Layer Builder's path to the solution (fewest places first, starting from the givens), how many layers had more than one possible placement — the real decisions. The rest were forced.
Layers · your method / singles
How many numbers have all their cells filled by logic alone, from the givens: with your layer method (box with one open cell, then rows/columns missing one number), or with singles in all four cube directions.
Needs guessing, Made Easy / Hard
Share of puzzles that singles can't finish; share made at the Easy or Hard level.
Logic %
Share of empty cells that singles (one place in a line, or one number for a cell) can fill before any guessing.
Go-backs, placements tried
How hard the Layer Builder had to search.
Givens, top repeat
Number of givens; how many times the most repeated number is given.
Classic %
Share of puzzles whose solution is in the home grid's family.
